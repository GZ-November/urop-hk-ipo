## 1. Demand-Side Secondary Turnover Evolution: HKEX Cash Market Transaction Surveys (`CMTS`, 2000/01–2020)

HKEX conducted its flagship *Cash Market Transaction Survey* (tracking Exchange Participant agency & principal turnover; no 2017 edition) through **2020** (published April 2022). Two structural transformations stand out across two decades:
1. **Retail-to-Institutional Transition & Persistent Overseas Share**: Local HK investors fell from roughly half of cash turnover in `2000/01` to **`30.7%`** in `2020` (`20.9%` local institutional, `9.8%` local retail), while **overseas investors consistently contributed `~40%–46%`** of total market turnover (`41.2%` in 2020), and Exchange Participants' principal trading climbed as algorithmic/program trading grew (`28.1%` in 2020).
2. **Geographic Rotation Within Overseas Capital**: In `2000/01`, **UK (`33%` of overseas)** and **US (`29%` of overseas)** institutions supplied **`62%`** of all foreign order flow, while **Mainland China was just `3%`**. By `2016` (after Shanghai & Shenzhen Connect launches), **Mainland China surged to `22%`** of overseas trading—matching the UK (`23%`) and overtaking the US (`20%`). By the final **`2020` survey**, **Asia (dominated by Mainland China via Southbound Connect, `8.6%` of total market turnover on its own) reached `48%` of overseas trading (`19.7%` of all HKEX cash turnover)**, nearly matching Europe (`24%` of overseas / `10.0%` of market) and the US (`23%` of overseas / `9.6%` of market) combined!

**Key Structural Synthesis for Section 11.1**:
Comparing **primary-market intermediation** (`PRC sponsors 48.9% -> 90.8%`, `Rule 144A large-deal tranche falling from 62.0% to 40.8%`, US/EU sponsors `45.6% -> 25.6%`) against **secondary trading** (`Southbound Connect climbing from 6% -> 23%` of market ADT, while `40%+` of 2020 cash turnover remained non-HK overseas) and **asset management** (`63%` non-HK and `>54%` ex-HK/ex-Mainland in Hong Kong's `HK$42.2T` 2025 AUM pool) resolves a widespread misconception: **the Sinification of Hong Kong's IPO underwriting syndicate (`90.8%` PRC sponsors) ran far ahead of—and did not eliminate—international asset-management and global secondary participation**, even as U.S. institutional `144A` tranche inclusion dropped 21 percentage points after 2022 and Mainland Southbound liquidity rose to nearly a quarter of daily cash turnover.

**Why International Issuers Struggled Beyond Day-1 (`Section 11.3` Diagnosis)**:
Hong Kong offered attractive **initial Asia-story multiples and retail/cornerstone validation** in `2010–2011`, *not* deep home-market secondary liquidity for non-Chinese operating assets. Once listed, overseas issuers encountered (a) **exclusion from Southbound Stock Connect** (historically limited to Hang Seng Composite mainland/HK constituents before cautious international-inclusion expansions), (b) **home-bias and register fragmentation** (`0.3%` HK share registry at Glencore), and (c) **valuation discounts during China risk-off cycles**—triggering voluntary delistings (`Glencore 2018, Coach 2018`), founder take-privates (`L'Occitane 2024`), or Western dual-listing pivots (`Samsonite, Ferretti, Prada`).
