# Referee report source checks

## Refereeing and empirical writing

- JFE sets a maximum of three essential points and asks reviewers to leave discretionary suggestions to authors. JF asks for prioritized publication hurdles and close attention to the identifying variation and whether an empirical strategy rules out competing stories. [JFE referee guidelines](https://www.jfinec.com/referees), [JF referee guidelines](https://afajof.org/wp-content/uploads/files/policies-and-guidelines/JF_Referee_Guidelines.pdf)
- Berk, Harvey, and Hirshleifer ask referees to explain material concerns, tie requests to evidence, weigh the cost of additional tests, and respect the authors’ decisions on secondary matters. Cochrane recommends opening with the central finding, showing the facts behind it, and explaining the variation that identifies each estimate and its economic magnitude. [Berk et al. (2017)](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jep.31.1.231), [Cochrane’s writing notes](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/phd_paper_writing.pdf)

## Hong Kong listing rules and implications for the manuscript

- The August 2025 amendments apply to applicants with listing documents published on or after August 4. The package also introduced a 40% initial minimum for the institutional bookbuilding tranche and changed requirements for initial public and free float. These contemporaneous provisions can also affect Day-1 volume and price. The Main Board historical RD code uses a listing-date cutoff. The modern panel correctly uses prospectus publication and records 2627.HK as a legacy deal even though it listed after August 4. [HKEX announcement of August 1, 2025](https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2025/2508012news?sc_lang=en), [historical RD filter](../../../analysis/run_referee2_remediation.py#L431), [modern cutoff](../../../analysis/clean_2025_2026_micropanel_and_rock.py#L329)
- Legacy PN18 prescribed a 10% minimum initial public subscription tranche and 30%, 40%, and 50% allocations at demand thresholds of 15, 50, and 100 times initial allocation. Mechanism A uses 5%, 15%, 25%, and 35%. Mechanism B permits 10% to 60% initially and has no mandatory demand-triggered clawback. During August 2025–April 2026 the government reported 95 B deals with 10% initially and 10%–15% finally, two A deals at 35% finally, and 12 Chapter 18C bespoke deals. HKEX grants waivers and Chapter 18C is governed by Rule 18C.09. The contemporary observations offer a useful cross-check after reconciling sample coverage. [HKEX revised listing guide](https://en-rules.hkex.com.hk/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/Guide_for_New_Listing_Applicants_%28blacklined%29_%28EN%29_202508.pdf), [revised PN18](https://en-rules.hkex.com.hk/node/3737/revisions/38935/view), [government reply to the Legislative Council](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202605/13/P2026051200604.htm)
- The modern file labels six 2026Q2 issues Mechanism A even though their prospectuses state Mechanism B. Correcting those six alone changes B adoption from 142/151 (94.0%) to 148/151 (98.0%). The treatment of bespoke schedules also needs review. WeRide (0800.HK), labeled A, launched with a 5% retail allocation (4,412,500 of 88,250,000 shares) and used a bespoke 10% and 20% clawback schedule at 10x and 50x (reaching 73.44x subscription and 17,650,000 final retail shares = 20.0% in its allotment announcement and local 2025Q4 JSONs). The two remaining statutory Mechanism A deals in the post-reform sample—SICC (2631.HK; local prospectus PDF pp. 388–390) and AUX Electric (2580.HK; local prospectus PDF pp. 412–415)—explicitly specify Paragraph 4.2(a) of Practice Note 18 (5% initial rising to 15%/25%/35% at 15x/50x/100x). The panel’s fallback rule uses final public allocation above 14% to assign A, which is incompatible with permissible B allocations and with A’s 5% initial public subscription tranche. The subsequent diagnostic’s group labeled A includes both the 9 labeled A and 23 Chapter 18C observations. The apparent A+H comparator rate of 9.4% comes from this mixed group. The 9 currently labeled A observations have a 22.2% rate before correcting the labels. - HKEX’s price constraint follows the active offering document. An increase above the top requires cancellation and a relaunch at the revised price with a supplemental or new prospectus. HKEX adopted a qualified downward flexibility mechanism in 2018 and declined to introduce the proposed upward flexibility mechanism in 2025. Observing no breach of the active offer range is compatible with completion-based selection or wide initial ranges. A within-book ceiling should be distinguished from an estimate of the latent price absent the constraint. [HKEX December 2024 consultation, para. 272](https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/News/Market-Consultations/2016-Present/December-2024-Optimise-IPO-Price/Consultation-Paper/cp202412.pdf), [2018 guidance announcement](https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2018/1802022news?sc_lang=en), [2025 decision](https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2025/2508012news?sc_lang=en)
- Section 7.1 describes a launch-to-pricing market shock. The builder computes the Hang Seng return from the prospectus date to the trading day before listing, potentially including public information arriving after the offer price is fixed. The regression also does not condition on an observed price range or prior room for price adjustment. Consequently, the coefficient can reflect the ordinary response of the first trading price to later public news. [Market return window](../../../analysis/build_hkma_hibor_and_144a_panel.py#L408), [return regressions](../../../analysis/run_hibor_fini_144a_and_bessembinder_econometrics.py#L213)
- FINI shortened the pricing-to-trading timetable from five to two business days and applies to applicants with a prospectus published on or after November 22, 2023. The SFC distinguishes the absence of pre-ballot transfers to issuers by clearing participants from underlying client deposits and financing. A compressed amount for bank confirmation applies if the participant opts in. The SFC’s 90% rule for recognizing IPO subscription receivables in liquid assets is not a universal financing rate. Code assigns FINI by listing date and pools a demand-derived capital proxy across both funding regimes.
