# Hong Kong domestic value: additions to the IPO history blueprint

Research date: 17 November 2026. This is a source-backed editorial research note, not a completed causal impact study. It does not alter the ordinary-equity IPO census.

## Central missing question

The existing blueprint follows issuers and investors closely. A fuller history should also follow the host city: what income, skills, institutions, public revenue, household opportunities, and distributional conflicts remained in Hong Kong after securities were sold? A dedicated section, **What the Market Built in Hong Kong**, can connect these channels without equating the entire financial sector with IPO activity.

## Eight primary-source starting points

### 1. The monetary foundation: the 1983 linked exchange rate

The HKMA dates the Linked Exchange Rate System to 17 October 1983; its present framework maintains the currency within HK$7.75–7.85 per US dollar. [HKMA, Linked Exchange Rate System](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/money/linked-exchange-rate-system/).

Add **The Currency behind the Capital Market** to the early history. Explain why investors care about settlement currency, convertibility, funding liquidity, and monetary credibility before they evaluate an issuer. Connect this historical foundation to later HIBOR and FINI sections. The contribution to IPO competitiveness is a mechanism to investigate, not an estimated causal result. Do not project today's precise band backwards over the entire post-1983 period or imply that a currency peg removes equity risk.

### 2. MTR: public ownership becomes listed ownership

The government-hosted extract of MTR's 2000 prospectus identifies the offering as a global sale by the Financial Secretary Incorporated on behalf of the government, with an initial one billion offer shares. [MTR Privatisation Share Offer, original prospectus extract](https://gia.info.gov.hk/general/201204/25/P201204250310_0310_92924.pdf).

Add a case on **Public Services, Public Shareholders**: who received sale proceeds; how government control, minority investors, service obligations, and commercial incentives coexisted. This prevents the narrative from implying that every IPO directly injected new money into an operating company. Follow the proceeds and distinguish selling-shareholder receipts from primary corporate capital.

### 3. MTR: a concrete household participation story

A contemporaneous government announcement reported distribution of 3.4 million application forms over the first two offer days. That is a count of forms, not applications, investors, or households. [Government, 26 September 2000](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/200009/26/0926236.htm).

Use this to introduce **From Paper Forms to Mobile Applications**: the changing daily experience of investing, bank queues, application logistics, household access, and the distinction between participation and successful wealth accumulation. Verify actual unique applicants/allottees from allocation notices before quantitative comparisons with today's app users.

### 4. Tracker Fund: intervention, exit, and retail diversification

HKMA's account explains how the government sought to dispose of shares bought during the 1998 intervention without disrupting the market. The 1999 Tracker Fund launch raised HK$33.3 billion from more than 180,000 retail investors and institutions. [HKMA, 10th Anniversary of Listing of TraHK](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/insight/2009/11/20091112/).

Add **From Crisis Intervention to Broad Share Ownership** to Epoch I. This case illustrates the financial market's capacity to unwind state intervention and offer diversified participation. It is an ETF/unit trust and belongs in the institutional narrative, not in the ordinary operating-company IPO denominator. Its proceeds were not fresh corporate investment in all constituent companies.

### 5. Link REIT: public assets and the contested meaning of value

The government yearbook records the Housing Authority's fiscal and public-housing rationale, the unsuccessful 2004 launch following legal proceedings, and the eventual listing on 25 November 2005. It states that the Housing Authority retained no equity interest in Link or its manager after divestment. [Hong Kong 2005, Divestment of Commercial Properties](https://www.yearbook.gov.hk/2005/en/11_05.htm).

Use **Who Benefits When Public Assets Become Securities?** to examine the government's balance sheet, asset management, investors, tenants, and neighbourhood services. Link is a REIT and should remain outside ordinary-equity statistics. A balanced case should subsequently obtain tenant/rental outcomes and competing contemporary testimony; the government's stated objective is not evidence that all social objectives were achieved.

### 6. The size of the host financial-services economy

C&SD's Four Key Industries table reports financial-services value added of HK$816.2 billion in 2024, 26.2% of GDP, and 265,000 jobs, 7.2% of employment. These are sector-wide figures, including banking and insurance. [C&SD, Table 655-82101](https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/web_table.html?id=655-82101).

Build a host-city dashboard covering financial-services employment, compensation, professional services, and cross-border service exports over time. These figures establish context; they are not estimates of jobs or GDP caused by IPOs. Legal/accounting services may sit in other sectors, so adding broad sector totals would double-count or over-attribute activity.

### 7. HKEX's business model: recurring value after listing

HKEX's 2024 financial statements distinguish trading, clearing, custody, market data, and listing income. Group-wide listing fees were HK$1.484 billion, while trading fees and tariffs were HK$7.189 billion; those group totals include products and geographies beyond Hong Kong ordinary equities. [HKEX Annual Report 2024, income statement and revenue notes](https://www.hkexgroup.com/-/media/HKEX-Group-Site/ssd/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Reports/documents/2025/250317ar_e.pdf).

Add **The Economic Life of a Listing**: IPO fees initiate a relationship that can generate subsequent trading, settlement, custody, disclosure, audit, and financing services. Use segment and product notes to isolate relevant income; do not attribute the entire exchange group's revenue to IPOs or assume all group activity is produced in Hong Kong.

### 8. Measurement foundation: proceeds, output, and national income

C&SD defines value added as gross output less intermediate consumption. GDP aggregates production within the economy; GNI additionally adjusts for primary income flows between residents and non-residents. [C&SD, National Accounts concepts](https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/scode250.html).

Make **What Counts as Hong Kong's Gain?** a compact methods box. Keep separate: corporate funds raised; service fees earned; domestic value added; government fiscal receipts; household investment returns; and issuer investment conducted in Hong Kong, Mainland China, or third countries. Gross proceeds and stock-price appreciation are not themselves GDP. Provider fee estimates need residency and intermediate-input adjustments before interpretation as Hong Kong value added.

## Recommended new exhibits and follow-up research

1. **A dollar raised in Hong Kong: where does it go?** Track investors, issuers/selling shareholders, local and overseas service providers, taxes, and eventual investment locations. Initially present a conceptual flow chart; populate only measured links.
2. **Public listing, different beneficiaries:** compare MTR, Tracker Fund, and Link across instrument, seller, proceeds recipient, retained state ownership, citizen access, and public-service obligations. Keep scope flags explicit.
3. **The listing services lifecycle:** distinguish one-off issuance revenue from recurring issuer, investor, and infrastructure services. Pair prospectus costs with provider accounts and sector statistics.
4. **Household access and distribution:** track genuine investor counts, allocation probabilities, financing costs, diversified versus concentrated participation, and returns. Form distribution, brokerage accounts, and unique people are different measures.
5. **Social value and trade-offs:** examine whether government fiscal gains, issuer financing, investor returns, and consumer welfare moved together. Do not collapse these into a single success ranking.

The strongest addition is a synthesis chapter rather than more isolated milestones: **Hong Kong as Host: Income, Institutions, and the Distribution of Financial Gains**. Place it after the market-mechanism chapters so that its contribution claims can draw on the preceding evidence.
